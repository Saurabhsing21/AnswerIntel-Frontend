**ChatGPT usually recommends a competitor because the competitor appears on more of the pages it reads, is described more consistently as the answer to the question, or is simply better known in its training data.** It is rarely about your product being worse. Each reason leaves a trace you can check.

## The five reasons

| Reason | How to check | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1. They are on the cited pages, you are not | Look at sources cited in the answer | Get onto those pages: outreach, listings, community answers |
| 2. They rank for the question, you do not | Search the question in Google and Bing | Publish a page that answers that exact question |
| 3. They fit the question's wording better | Compare how the question and their pages describe the use case | Describe your product in the buyer's words |
| 4. More of the web mentions them | Search their brand name vs yours | Earn mentions: launches, reviews, directories, PR |
| 5. The model learned them in training | Ask with search off | Long-term mention building; wait for new models |

## Mentioned but not recommended

Often you are named in passing ("other options include...") while a competitor is the pick. That usually means the sources mention you but do not argue for you. Pages that say who you are best for, with specifics, turn mentions into recommendations. Track mention rate and recommendation rate separately; see [what AI search visibility is](/blog/what-is-ai-search-visibility).

## FAQ

### Is ChatGPT biased toward big brands?

It favours brands the web discusses most, which tends to be larger ones, especially in answers from memory. Search-based answers give smaller brands a faster way in through the pages that rank.

### Will improving my own website fix it?

Partly. Your site helps for questions where it ranks. For "best" questions, third-party pages usually matter more.
