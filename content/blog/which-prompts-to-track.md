**Track 30 to 50 real buyer questions to start: category questions ("best X for Y"), competitor questions ("X alternatives", "X vs Y"), problem questions, pricing questions and questions about your own brand.** Choose them from what buyers actually ask your sales team, in their words, not from your keyword list.

## The five kinds of prompts

| Type | Example | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Category | "Best CRM for a 5-person agency" | Where AI picks winners |
| Competitor | "HubSpot alternatives for small teams" | Buyers already shopping |
| Problem | "How do I stop losing leads in spreadsheets" | Early research, tool fits naturally |
| Pricing | "Cheapest CRM with email sequences" | High intent |
| Brand | "What is [your brand]? Is it good?" | What AI says when buyers check you |

A good starting split is about 40% category, 25% competitor, 20% problem, 10% pricing and 5% brand.

## How to find them

1. **Sales calls and support tickets:** the questions people asked before buying.
2. **Reddit and communities:** how people describe the problem.
3. **"People also ask" in Google** for your category.
4. **Ask the AI itself:** "What questions do buyers ask before choosing a [category]?"

## How many is enough

- **30 to 50** gives a stable picture for one product. Seenlense generates 30 to 50 from your category and competitors, and you edit them before the first scan (Seenlense is our product).
- **More prompts** help once you track several segments, countries or products.
- **Fewer than 15** is too noisy to read trends from.

## Keep the list stable

Change prompts rarely. If you rewrite the list every week, you cannot tell whether visibility changed or the questions did.

## FAQ

### How many prompts should I track?

30 to 50 for one product to start. Add more for extra segments or markets.

### Should I include my brand name in prompts?

Only in a few brand questions. Most prompts should be unbranded, because that is where AI decides whether to name you at all.
